Mission & Global Crisis

What is AquaSim?

AquaSim is an educational presentation framework that connects everyday home habits, such as a long shower, a running tap or a half-empty washing machine, to the wider systems of global water stress. It turns abstract statistics into numbers a person can recognise from their own day.

The project is built for classrooms, workshops and self-study. It is deliberately plain: every page is pure HTML, readable on any device, with screen readers and with no setup.

Start with the Water Footprint Analyzer, or jump to the Crisis Map to see where scarcity is most severe.

Solving the Global Water Crisis

Water stress is not solved at one level. It needs coordinated action at both scales.

Macro-infrastructure overhauls

  • Leak reduction: ageing municipal pipe networks lose a large share of treated water before it reaches a tap.
  • Wastewater reuse: treating and recycling urban wastewater for industry, irrigation and aquifer recharge.
  • Efficient agriculture: agriculture is the largest water user worldwide, so drip irrigation, soil-moisture sensing and drought-tolerant crops matter most.
  • Aquifer governance: metering and limits on groundwater extraction so aquifers are not drawn down faster than they refill.
  • Desalination and storage: carefully sited, energy-aware capacity and rainwater harvesting where supply is structurally short.

Micro-behavioral modifications

  • Shorter showers and turning taps off while brushing or washing up.
  • Running full laundry and dishwasher loads only.
  • Fixing dripping taps and running toilets promptly.
  • Choosing lower-water foods and goods (see the Data Vault).

Practical steps are listed on the Solutions page.

The Resource Empathy Bridge

People change habits when they can see the consequence of them. The Resource Empathy Bridge is AquaSim's core teaching idea, and it has three steps:

  1. Measure: estimate your own daily water use, action by action.
  2. Compare: set that figure beside the 15 litres per person per day commonly used as the minimum survival allowance in humanitarian refugee-camp response.
  3. Adapt: choose specific changes, track the difference and repeat.

Tracking data turns guilt into agency: once the numbers are visible, each small habit has a measurable effect, and that is what makes behaviour change stick.
