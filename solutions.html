How to Fix Everyday Waste

Strategies are sorted by effort: habits you can change today, and hardware upgrades that save water permanently.

Immediate lifestyle habit corrections

Bathroom

  • Keep showers to about five minutes; a timer helps.
  • Turn the tap off while brushing teeth, shaving or soaping hands.
  • Use a bucket to catch cold water while the shower warms up, and reuse it for plants or cleaning.

Kitchen and laundry

  • Run the washing machine and dishwasher only when full.
  • Wash vegetables in a bowl, not under a running tap, then reuse the water on plants.
  • Thaw food in the fridge, not under running water.

Food and shopping

  • Waste less food; every discarded item also wastes the water used to grow it.
  • Choose lower-water foods more often (see the Data Vault).
  • Repair and re-wear clothing before buying new.

Long-term low-flow hardware and plumbing fixes

  1. Find and fix leaks first. Add a few drops of food colouring to the toilet cistern; if colour reaches the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking.
  2. Check the water meter. Note the reading, use no water for an hour, then check again. Any change means a hidden leak.
  3. Install aerators on taps to cut flow while keeping pressure comfortable.
  4. Fit a low-flow showerhead that limits flow to roughly 6 to 9 litres per minute.
  5. Upgrade to a dual-flush toilet or fit a displacement device in an older cistern.
  6. Choose water-efficient appliances and check their rated water use per cycle.
  7. Add rainwater harvesting or greywater reuse for gardens and cleaning, where local rules allow.
  8. Move to drip irrigation and mulch for any garden or balcony plants.
