Comparative Water Footprint Metrics

Most of the water we use is invisible: it is spent growing, processing and shipping the things we eat and buy. This is called virtual water. The tables give global-average estimates; real values vary a lot with climate, farming method and region.

Reading the numbers

  • One cotton T-shirt carries the equivalent of roughly two to three years of drinking water for one person.
  • A single cup of coffee hides more than a hundred litres, mostly from growing the beans.
  • Animal products generally need far more water per kilogram than plant foods.
  • Wasting food wastes all the water embedded in it.

Compare these figures with the 15-litre camp allowance in the Analyzer, or turn them into action with the Solutions page.

Figures are rounded global averages compiled from water-footprint research such as work by Mekonnen and Hoekstra and the Water Footprint Network. Individual products vary widely; verify before citing.
