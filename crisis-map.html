Global Water Scarcity Directory

Water stress compares how much water is withdrawn with how much is renewably available. The table below gives a broad regional picture. Stress varies widely inside every region, so treat each row as a headline, not a verdict on any one country or city.

Reading the stress levels

  • Low: withdrawals are a small share of available supply.
  • Medium: withdrawals are a moderate share and local shortages can appear in dry years.
  • High: a large share of supply is already used, leaving little buffer.
  • Extreme: withdrawals approach or exceed renewable supply; drought quickly becomes crisis.

What drives scarcity?

Aquifer depletion
Groundwater pumped faster than rainfall can refill it, so wells fall and the land can sink.
Drought and climate change
Hotter, less predictable rainfall and shrinking snow and glacier stores reduce reliable supply.
Agricultural demand
Irrigation accounts for the majority of global freshwater withdrawals.
Pollution
Contaminated water is unusable water, shrinking the effective supply.
Infrastructure gaps
Leaky networks, missing storage and inadequate treatment waste or block access to available water.
