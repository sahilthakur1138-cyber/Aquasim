Interactive Presentation Guide & Quiz

For educators running a live session with AquaSim. The agenda below fits a 45-minute class; adjust the timings to suit your group.

Lecture agenda

  1. Hook (5 min): ask the class to guess how many litres they used this morning. Record the guesses. Open the Home page.
  2. The global picture (8 min): walk through the Crisis Map. Ask which regions surprise students and why.
  3. Measure it (10 min): students complete the Water Footprint Analyzer by hand and compare totals with the 15-litre camp allowance.
  4. Hidden water (7 min): use the Data Vault to discuss virtual water in food and clothing.
  5. Act (5 min): in pairs, pick three changes from the Solutions page and estimate the litres saved.
  6. Check understanding (10 min): students complete the quiz below.

Discussion prompts

  • Why does a country with plenty of rainfall still import water-intensive goods?
  • Who should bear the cost of fixing leaking public pipes?
  • What would you give up first if you had only 15 litres a day?

Reading comprehension check

Question 1. What daily water allowance is commonly used as a minimum in emergency refugee camps?

Question 2. Which sector is the largest user of freshwater worldwide?

Question 3. Which of these are quick habit changes that save water? (Select all that apply.)

Question 4. Which of these are causes of regional water scarcity? (Select all that apply.)

Question 5. What does "virtual water" mean?

Teacher answer key

Show answers and explanations
  1. 15 litres per person. This reflects the humanitarian minimum for drinking, cooking and basic hygiene.
  2. Agriculture. Irrigation accounts for the majority of global freshwater withdrawals.
  3. Turning the tap off, shortening showers, and washing vegetables in a bowl. Running the washing machine half-empty wastes water.
  4. Aquifer depletion, prolonged drought and leaking infrastructure. Many lakes would generally ease scarcity.
  5. The water used to produce a good or food item, hidden in the product.
